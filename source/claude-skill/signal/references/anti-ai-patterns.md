# Signal: AI-pattern editorial field guide

**Scope:** a high-precision checklist of habits commonly associated with overproduced prose. **Not an AI-origin detector.** Every pattern can occur in human writing. Correct a passage only when doing so makes it clearer, truer, or more individual.

## A. Padded entry and exit

1. **Era-setting clichés:** "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" without a real date or change.
2. **Permission to explain:** "It's important to note" before a mundane point.
3. **Essay roadmap filler:** "In this article, we will explore" when structure makes it obvious.
4. **Generic wrap-up:** "In conclusion" followed by an unchanged summary.
5. **False all-purpose takeaway:** "Ultimately, it comes down to" when a real constraint is missing.
6. **Over-signposting:** "Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally" in every paragraph.
7. **Anemic topic sentence:** "There are several key factors" before an unranked list.

## B. Abstract verbal inflation

8. **Hype verbs:** unlock, revolutionize, supercharge, elevate, transform, leverage without measurable change.
9. **Decorative adjectives:** robust, seamless, comprehensive, cutting-edge, powerful, dynamic without technical criteria.
10. **Empty mission nouns:** excellence, innovation, empowerment, synergy without who/what/outcome.
11. **Nominalization pileups:** implementation of the optimization of… rather than who changes what.
12. **Consultant verbs:** operationalize, facilitate, drive alignment when a concrete verb works.
13. **Metaphor drift:** ecosystem, journey, tapestry, landscape, paradigm, north star stacked together.
14. **Unqualified absolutes:** always, never, everyone, best-in-class where evidence is absent.
15. **Overpromising:** "will ensure" when the method can only support or increase the odds.

## C. Mechanically tidy rhetoric

16. **Formulaic contrast:** repeated "not just X, but Y" or "It's not X; it's Y" absent a meaningful distinction.
17. **Forced tricolons:** every paragraph enumerates three equally abstract benefits.
18. **Mirror-sentence construction:** too many sentences with identical syntax/length.
19. **Manufactured suspense:** "The real secret?" or "Here's the twist" without a genuine turn.
20. **Unnecessary rhetorical questions:** asking then answering what could be said directly.
21. **Inflated stakes:** "The future of X depends on" without evidence.
22. **Performative certainty:** "The truth is" where the claim is an inference.
23. **Synthetic aphorisms:** crisp one-line slogans that obscure rather than sharpen reasoning.

## D. Unauthentic social behavior

24. **Sycophantic praise:** "Great question!" or "Brilliant insight" as unearned filler.
25. **Imitated empathy:** unsolicited emotional reassurance in practical tasks.
26. **Forced friendliness:** slang, emojis, profanity, or cheeky intimacy that the author wouldn't use.
27. **Generic leadership voice:** everyone sounds like a keynote speaker rather than a credible colleague.
28. **Borrowed lived experience:** "I've seen firsthand" with no author-supplied basis.
29. **Overexplaining obvious constraints:** lecturing a domain expert on their own work.
30. **Artificially perfect balance:** every thesis yields two symmetrical sides even when evidence differs.

## E. Formatting and rhythm

31. **Em-dash dependence:** repeated interruptions substitute for actual sentence structure. Em dashes are allowed.
32. **Colon overload:** every sentence announces a framework.
33. **Bold-everything formatting:** typography stands in for prioritization.
34. **Nested bullet proliferation:** a short point becomes a hierarchy of trivial subpoints.
35. **Over-short paragraph pattern:** a punchy sentence on every line merely to simulate gravitas.
36. **Uniform paragraph cadence:** mechanically identical paragraph lengths, pace, and closure.
37. **Template bleed:** needless "Key takeaways" / "Why this matters" headings in personal prose.
38. **Parenthetical qualifications everywhere:** uncertainty should be integrated where it matters.

## F. Reasoning failures that often survive stylistic rewrites

39. **Absent actor:** "stakeholders will be aligned" but nobody is responsible.
40. **Absent mechanism:** outcomes asserted without explaining the pathway.
41. **Data-free specificity:** precise-sounding percentages with no supporting source.
42. **Invented consensus:** "experts agree" or "research shows" without evidence.
43. **Flattened uncertainty:** hypothesis rewritten as decision, risk as outcome, goal as result.
44. **False equivalence:** contrasting positions framed as equally supported without examining evidence.
45. **Causal leap:** sequence treated as proof of causation.
46. **Circular benefit claim:** "improves efficiency by streamlining processes".
47. **Attribution laundering:** an idea expressed as a quote or sourced statement without provenance.
48. **Audience mismatch:** conversational blog voice in a safety decision, or bureaucratic tone in a personal note.

## G. Overfitting traps — never make these mechanical rules

- Frequent em dashes, lists, perfectly grammatical prose, Latinate vocabulary, or formal language **do not establish AI generation**.
- A technical specification can and should repeat terms precisely.
- A human author may deliberately use tricolons, contrast, bolding, metaphors, or rhetorical questions.
- A strong editor doesn't require shorter text if length carries nuance or evidence.
- Artificial "humanization" (typos, random slang, eccentric punctuation, fake anecdotes, token substitutions) is worse than straightforward writing.

## Editorial intervention levels

- **Leave:** the structure is purposeful or author's voice depends on it.
- **Trim:** delete words with no information.
- **Translate:** replace abstractions with actors, actions, stakes, evidence and boundaries.
- **Restructure:** reorganize when formulaic structure hides the main point.
- **Query:** ask for missing facts, voice preferences or evidence rather than inventing them.
