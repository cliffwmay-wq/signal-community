# Semantic fidelity and editorial acceptance gates

## Gate 1 — meaning inventory

Before editing, note (mentally or in scratch): people, roles, actors, timeline, numbers, exact names, obligations, risks, constraints, exceptions, evidence, decisions, dissent and competing alternatives.

## Gate 2 — line-by-line semantic comparison

After editing, examine:

1. Did **every factual claim** remain accurate relative to its source?
2. Did a speculation become a certainty, goal become an achieved outcome, example become a rule, correlation become causation, or possibility become a commitment?
3. Were any negations, conditions, time ranges, quantities, dependencies, or scope limits lost?
4. Did attribution or a citation change meaning?
5. Was any dissent, important objection, or rejected option silently omitted?
6. Did technical vocabulary remain precise (e.g., "event stream" vs. "database")?
7. Does the author still sound like the author, at the intended level of formality?
8. Was any generated detail or unsupported authority inserted?
9. Are disclosures, limitations and provenance notes preserved if they are relevant to the publication context?

## Gate 3 — editorial integrity

- Keep genuinely good phrases, even if a classifier might flag their shape.
- Don't try to beat detectors; pursue clarity, natural rhythm, sound reasoning and accurate attribution.
- Don't insert random typos or quirks to imitate a human.
- Flag unresolved ambiguities instead of hallucinating a fix.

## Optional handoff output

When the request is for a handoff, structure it around: intent, context, settled decisions, reasons, constraints, unresolved questions, risks, next actions and evidence references. This is a knowledge object, not a cleaned transcript.
